## Overview

A rule-based trading strategy is an explicit mapping from information to actions. The value of rules is not that they are perfect; it is that they can be tested, debugged, and improved without ambiguity.

A strategy that cannot be described precisely cannot be evaluated reliably.

### Learning objectives

- Convert a hypothesis into executable entry and exit rules.
- Add realistic constraints, costs, and sizing.
- Validate a strategy with robustness checks, not just headline returns.

### The strategy specification

A minimal specification includes:

- Universe: which symbols are eligible.
- Signal: how you decide when to enter and exit.
- Portfolio rule: how you size positions and allocate capital.
- Execution rule: when and how trades are placed (next bar, close, open).
- Risk rules: stop logic, exposure caps, and drawdown controls.

### Example rule set (illustrative)

```
Entry: Close > SMA(50) and RSI(14) between 50 and 65
Exit:  Close < SMA(20) or RSI(14) > 75
Sizing: Equal-weight, max 10 positions
Costs:  Fees + slippage per trade
```

### Validation checklist (what to test)

- Costs and delays: do results survive fees, slippage, and execution delay?
- Concentration: are returns dominated by a few names or one sector?
- Stability: do small parameter changes break performance?
- Regimes: does the strategy fail in specific market conditions?
- Turnover and capacity: is it realistic for the intended capital size?

### Practical checklist

- Are all rules unambiguous and implementable?
- Is the time horizon consistent across signal, execution, and holding period?
- Are constraints encoded (max weight, liquidity filter, turnover cap)?
- Is there a plan for monitoring and de-risking after deployment?
